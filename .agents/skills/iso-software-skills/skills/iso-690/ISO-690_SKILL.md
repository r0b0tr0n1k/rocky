# ISO 690:2010 Bibliographic References

Follow ISO 690:2010 "Information and documentation — Guidelines for bibliographic references and citations to information resources" when creating references.

## Element Order

Standard order for all references:
1. Creator(s) name(s)
2. Title
3. Medium designation (if not print)
4. Edition
5. Production information (place: publisher)
6. Date
7. Series title and number
8. Numeration (volume, issue, pages)
9. Standard identifier (ISBN, ISSN, DOI)
10. Availability/access/location
11. Additional general information

For name-and-date (Harvard) system, insert year after creator.

## Creator Names

- Format: SURNAME, Forename(s) or initials
- Example: CRANE, D. or BACH, C.P.E.
- Two/three creators: give all names
- Four+ creators: first creator + "and others" or "et al."
- Organizations: use full form as shown on resource
- Anonymous: use "Anon" in name-and-date system

## Titles

- Use form as shown on the resource
- Translate if needed (original in brackets)
- Distinguish contribution title from host item with "In:" or typographic distinction
- Serials: use key title with ISSN; may abbreviate per ISO 4

## Dates

- Year sufficient for most resources
- Electronic resources: include access date [viewed YYYY-MM-DD]
- Patents: application and publication dates
- Format: YYYY-MM-DD or as shown on resource

## Electronic Resources

Required elements:
- Medium designation: [online], [CD], [DVD], etc.
- Date of citation: [viewed YYYY-MM-DD]
- Availability: Available from: URL or DOI
- System requirements if needed

## Citation Methods

1. **Name and date (Harvard)**: (Author YYYY) in text; list alphabetically by author
2. **Numeric**: [1], [2] in text; list numbered in citation order
3. **Running notes**: Superscript notes with full references

## Resource-Specific Rules

### Books/Monographs
`Creator. Title [medium]. Edition. Place: Publisher, Date. Series, Identifier.`

### Serials
`Title [medium]. Place: Publisher, Date-. ISSN.`

### Contributions
`Creator. Title. In: Host item title. Place: Publisher, Date, pages.`

### Patents
`Assignee. Title. Country Patent number. Date. Int. Cl. class.`

### Electronic Messages
`Creator. Title [electronic mail]. Date sent. Available from: address.`

### Audiovisual/Films
`Title [film/video]. Director. Place: Publisher, Date.`

### Maps
`Creator. Title. Scale. Place: Publisher, Date. Series designation.`

## Punctuation

- Use consistent punctuation throughout reference list
- Separate elements with periods
- Use commas within elements (page ranges, multiple creators)
- Brackets for supplied information, medium, dates not on resource

## Examples

**Book:**
CRANE, D. Invisible Colleges. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1972.

**Journal article:**
STIEG, M.F. The information needs of historians. College and Research Libraries. 1981, 42(6), 549-560. ISSN 0010-0870.

**Electronic resource:**
KAFKA, Franz. The Trial [online]. Translated by David WYLLIE. Project Gutenberg, 2005. Updated 2006-03-08 [viewed 5 June 2006]. Available from: http://www.gutenberg.org/...

**Patent:**
PHILIP MORRIS INC. Optical perforating apparatus and system. European patent application 0021165 A1. 1981-01-07.
